## Design Specificity Verdict

The Midnight Cartographer system is strongly product-authored: indigo field, earned gold preference state, topology-first canvas, and semantic filter colors fit an Atlas planning tool. The operating model needs to become as specific as the visual system.

The deterministic scan reported 12 advisory findings: 11 undocumented colors in custom-tag and legend code plus one undocumented 9px tag-removal control. The repeated cyan findings are contextual custom-tag tokens rather than independent defects. Objective interaction gaps remain: mouse/wheel-only pan and zoom, no Escape or focus transfer for the inspector, no tag-input label, unlabeled add-tag button, 32px controls, and no explicit loading, error, or empty states.

## Overall Impression

The opening is atmospheric and appropriately tactical; selecting a map creates a satisfying focused reading surface. The biggest opportunity is orienting players and reducing the number of simultaneous planning decisions.

## What's Working

- Gold remains reserved for durable preference and identity, so the preference state feels meaningful.
- The compact rail and shallow header protect the Atlas as the workspace's primary element.
- The inspector presents map facts, ratings, tags, and boss details with clear internal hierarchy.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Filter semantics and state are opaque

**Why it matters:** Players cannot tell whether a filter narrows, dims, or highlights the map, nor how many maps match.  
**Fix:** State “Highlight matching maps,” show a matching-map count, provide Clear filters, and make legend items removable controls.  
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The graph has no orientation or recovery controls

**Why it matters:** Panning and zooming can strand players in empty space, while the gestures are undiscoverable.  
**Fix:** Add compact reset-view and zoom controls plus a first-use “Scroll to zoom · drag to pan” cue.  
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

### [P1] The filter rail overloads the first decision

**Why it matters:** Eight peer filters exceed working-memory limits.  
**Fix:** Group filters by planning intent, keeping three to four common choices visible and disclosing the rest.  
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] The inspector can obscure too much of the map

**Why it matters:** The desktop inspector overlays active terrain and the mobile sheet can cover the legend.  
**Fix:** Add a collapsible summary or reserve desktop space; use a draggable mobile summary state and reposition the legend when open.  
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Preference and tag actions lack reassurance

**Why it matters:** Planning decisions need to feel persistently saved.  
**Fix:** Confirm changes inline, prevent or explain duplicate tags, and provide short undo after removal.  
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User):** No reset, search, shortcut, preset, or bulk preference workflow; filters cannot be cleared decisively.

**Jordan (First-Timer):** No explanation of panning, zooming, label changes, ring meanings, or whether non-matching maps remain interactive.

## Minor Observations

- Mobile horizontal filter scrolling has weak discoverability and can hide active state.
- The map/boss imagery can make inspector height volatile.
- Low-contrast supporting text may disappear on dim displays.
